Why the widget keeps saying "offline"

job-doot iPhone widget · investigated 16 Nov 2026 by 3 independent agents · nothing was changed

In one line: The Pi is fine. It answers in a few milliseconds and never failed a widget request. The widget says "offline" because your phone stops sending requests. iOS puts the Tailscale VPN to sleep in the background, and the widget's background refresh can't reach the Pi.

1. How the widget works (and where it breaks)

Every ~15-30 min iOS wakes the Scriptable widget in the background | v Widget asks: http://100.66.138.76:8080/api/summary (the Pi's Tailscale address) | v Request must go through the Tailscale VPN on the phone <-- BREAKS HERE | v Pi answers in ~0.01 s with your numbers --> rings drawn

When the Tailscale tunnel on the phone is asleep, the request never leaves the phone. After 10 seconds the widget gives up and draws the "offline - Tailscale off or Pi down" card. iOS then decides on its own when to try again, which can be hours later, so the card stays stuck.

Update 23:07: your phone confirmed it. The Tailscale app on the iPhone shows a warning: "DNS Unavailable: Tailscale can't reach the configured DNS servers" (dns-forward-failing).
  • Your tailnet has no custom DNS servers set, so Tailscale on the phone relies on the Wi-Fi router's own DNS. If it can't reach even the router, Tailscale's part of the phone has lost its own network access. That fits the dead tunnel to the Pi (0 handshakes).
  • The widget calls the Pi by IP address, so DNS itself isn't what blocks the widget. The warning is a symptom of the same stuck Tailscale connection on the phone.
  • Plausible extra trigger, not verified: your home router hands out an IPv6 "link-local" DNS address (fe80::…) as the first DNS server. This network already has a history of IPv6 timeouts (e.g. pi5.local), and Tailscale may fail to reach that kind of address.
  • Fix now: in the Tailscale app on the phone, turn the VPN off and on again.
    Lasting fix (free, no code): in the Tailscale admin console, open DNS, add a global nameserver (e.g. Cloudflare 1.1.1.1) and turn on "Override DNS servers". Tailscale then stops depending on the router's DNS.

Why tapping the widget seems to "work"

The Pi's logs show no dashboard page load from your phone today. Safari was most likely showing an old copy of the page saved from your last visit, not fresh data. Try it: pull down to refresh in Safari while the widget says offline. If the page fails or spins, the tunnel is down.

Ruled out

  • Slow server or busy Pi: replies take milliseconds and the CPU is 97% idle.
  • Database locks, wrong key, server errors: none in 3 days of logs (34 of 34 summary requests OK).
  • A daily cron job, deploy or restart breaking things: nothing lines up with the gaps.
  • Midnight or date bugs: requests succeeded across midnight.
  • Pi Wi-Fi or Pi Tailscale: stable (one 19-second Wi-Fi blip at 03:02, unrelated). The Pi's Tailscale key is valid until May 2027.

4. Why it looks like it "breaks every day"

  1. During the day the phone's Tailscale tunnel goes idle or gets killed by iOS. iOS gives VPN apps very little memory, and Tailscale's network extension gets killed when it goes over.
  2. The next background refresh can't reach the Pi, so the widget draws "offline".
  3. iOS treats "refresh in 15 min" as a suggestion only, so the stuck card can stay for hours.
  4. The widget throws away the last good numbers and shows the same "offline" text for every kind of failure, so you can't tell "phone can't reach Pi" from "Pi is dead".
  5. You open Scriptable or Tailscale, things wake up, it works for a few hours, and then the cycle repeats.

5. Possible solutions

Quick checks on the phone (free, 2 minutes)

  • Open the Tailscale app and confirm it says Connected. In its settings, turn on VPN On Demand (iOS then brings the tunnel back up automatically).
  • Update the Tailscale app. Recent releases fix the network extension running out of memory.
  • Low Power Mode cuts background refreshes. Expect more "offline" while it's on.

On their own these will likely help, but they won't fully fix it. iOS can still put the tunnel to sleep.

Option 1: make the widget honest (small code change) recommended

  • Save the last good numbers on the phone. On failure, keep showing them with a grey footer like last update 1:30 PM · can't reach Pi instead of a blank "offline".
  • Show the real reason: "phone can't reach Pi (VPN asleep)" vs "Pi replied with an error".
  • Skip re-downloading the widget code on every refresh (once every few hours is enough). This halves the network work per refresh.

Example: instead of a blank card at 6 PM, you'd see your 1:30 PM rings plus ● 1:30 PM · stale (VPN asleep). It's still useful and tells you exactly what's wrong.

Option 2: don't need the phone's VPN at all (Tailscale Funnel, free)

  • Tailscale Funnel can give one small Pi endpoint a public https://pi5.<tailnet>.ts.net address. It's free on your plan and needs no domain.
  • The widget calls that address instead of 100.66.138.76, so it works even when the phone's Tailscale is asleep or off.
  • Trade-off: that one endpoint becomes reachable from the internet, protected by the widget key. It only exposes read-only counts. Only the summary endpoint should be exposed, not the whole dashboard.

This is the only option that removes the root cause. Options 1 and 3 make the problem easier to live with.

Option 3: Pi pushes, phone doesn't pull

  • The Pi sends the numbers to something the phone already receives without a VPN (for example a free push service, or Telegram). This is a bigger change, and you've said you rarely open Telegram, so it's lower priority.

For yesterday's real outage

  • The Pi lost power or froze around 17:05 on 15 Nov. No under-voltage was recorded, so this was likely a power cut or the plug, not a weak adapter. A cheap UPS or power bank with pass-through would cover short power cuts.
